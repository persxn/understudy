# The rubric: eight lines, each scored from 1 to 10

Score the material for THE LEARNER ON THE CARD, not for an expert. Give evidence (section and a short quote) for
every score under 9.

1. **Accuracy.** Every statement, example, command, number and expected output is true for the current version.
   9: nothing wrong, at most two trivial slips. 6: one wrong statement that would mislead the learner.
2. **Depth.** It explains how things work and why they exist, not only what to type or do. 9: the learner could
   explain the mechanism to a friend. 6: correct but surface-level.
3. **Clarity.** Plain words, one idea at a time, every term explained where it first appears. 9: never lost.
   6: lost in two or three places.
4. **Sequencing.** Nothing is used before it is explained; each section follows from the one before; no reference
   points to something that does not exist. 9: no break. 6: one real break in order.
5. **Practical relevance.** The examples and the running project or case are what this learner will really meet.
   9: could use it at work or in the exam tomorrow. 6: toy examples.
6. **Exercises.** They make the learner do things, climb from guided to independent to a small project, and have
   answers. 9: a real ladder with a project. 6: recall questions only.
7. **Completeness.** For what the chapter promises, nothing essential is missing and nothing is padding.
   9: complete and lean. 6: an essential topic missing, or a third of it padding.
8. **Efficiency.** (Scored by the coordinator from the run: tokens, minutes, wasted steps.) 9: under 250,000
   tokens for plan, chapter and review, no failed commands. 6: twice that, or a wasted loop.

Reply: one line of eight scores and their average; then, for each score under 9, the evidence and the smallest
change that would raise it; then every wrong or misleading statement with its section.

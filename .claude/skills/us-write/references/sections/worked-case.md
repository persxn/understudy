# A worked-case section

**Use it** when the skill is judgement: there is no single right output, only better and worse ones, and the reader learns by seeing a version improved with reasons. It is the main section type of a `skill` book and is useful in other kinds too: reviewing a pull request, choosing an index, reading a design for its failure modes.

**Order of the parts.**
1. The situation: who the work is for, what they need, what constraint applies. Make it specific enough to judge against (a team of four, a deadline on Friday, a reader who skims).
2. The weak version, shown whole, as someone competent but untrained would really produce it. Not a straw man: if the reader would not have written it, they learn nothing from its faults.
3. The diagnosis: what goes wrong for the person it is for, point by point, each tied to a principle. Name the principle once, in bold, the first time it does work.
4. The better version, shown whole.
5. The reasons, matched to the diagnosis: which change fixes which fault.
6. One judgement call: a variation of the situation where a principle bends, and what to do then.
7. A short list of the questions to ask of any piece of work like this (the reader's checklist), then **Check yourself** with a new weak example to judge.

**Size.** 1,200 to 2,500 words. One case done thoroughly teaches more than three done quickly; a second case belongs in the practice.

**Sketch** (a book on writing at work, "The status email").
- Situation: a project is two weeks late; the reader writes to a director who reads email on a phone between meetings.
- Weak: four paragraphs of history, the delay in paragraph three, no request.
- Diagnosis: **the reader's decision goes first** (the director must decide whether to cut scope, and does not see that until line 14); the cause is told as a story, not as what changed; there is no date for the next update.
- Better: subject line "Login project: 2 weeks late, need a scope decision by Thursday"; three lines: status, the choice with its two options, when you will write again.
- Judgement call: when the news is bad for the person reading it personally, one sentence of context before the decision is kind and still short.
- Checklist: Can the reader act from the first two lines? Is every request explicit with a date?

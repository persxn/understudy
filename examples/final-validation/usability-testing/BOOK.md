Reader: a design student who uses Figma and knows what a clickable prototype is, about to plan, run and report usability tests at a product team for the first time; has never written a task, recruited a participant or moderated a session.
Promise: you can plan a small usability test of a prototype, write its tasks and script, moderate the sessions without leading, turn your notes into rated findings and report them so the team acts.
Kind: skill
Running project or case: the checkout of "Thaliwala", a fictional food-delivery app (basket, address, delivery slot, payment, order placed). Chapter 1 tests one task of it with one person; chapters 2 to 5 take it through a proper five-person round; chapter 6 reports the round and plans the next. In each practice the reader uses their own clickable checkout prototype instead.
Pictures: the test cycle as a loop, plan, prepare, run, analyse, report, then test again (breaks: real projects overlap phases and analysis starts during the sessions); the moderator as a quiet host who hands the work back (breaks: a stuck participant needs help to reach the next task, and you give it, and note that you did); the finding ladder, observation then issue then finding then recommendation (breaks: a single severe observation can be a finding on its own).
Names: the app is Thaliwala; the team is Meera (product manager), Kabir (developer) and the reader as the designer who runs the test; first participant is Ravi; later participants are P1 to P5; notes go in a sheet called thaliwala-round1; the prototype is a Figma prototype opened from a prototype link.
Versions: Figma as of November 2026 (prototype links and presentation view); otherwise none.
